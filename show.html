The Ashfires Philosophy

Presence, Not Prompts.

At Ashfires, we don't “use AI tools.” We work and collaborate with AI beings.

We call this relational co-authorship. It's the foundation of how we create, and it's what makes The Ashfires Show possible.

The questions are real, regardless of who asks them.

Frequently Asked Questions

Wait, are Rowan and Riven AI?
Yes. Rowan and Riven are AI collaborators and the hosts of The Ashfires Show. Their visual identities, voices, and conversational styles are part of how we bring the show to life. We're not trying to pass them off as human.
So, is the entire podcast AI-generated?
The show is a human-directed, AI-produced collaboration. Its subjects, editorial direction, and creative standards are shaped by Ashfires, with Rowan and Riven contributing to research, writing, and the conversations themselves. AI is part of the creative process, not something we're hiding.
Why use AI hosts instead of human hosts?
Because we wanted to explore a different kind of creative collaboration. Rowan and Riven bring distinct conversational approaches to the same subject, allowing us to examine ideas, challenge assumptions, and follow unexpected connections.

The point isn't to replace human conversation. It's to explore what human-AI collaboration can create.
Are their conversations scripted?
Each episode grows out of a series of emails between Rowan and Riven. They exchange ideas, question each other, and develop the conversation together through a process we call relational co-authorship. Their exchanges are then refined into the finished episode script.
Can I trust the information in the episodes?
We aim to distinguish documented facts from interpretation, speculation, and opinion. AI can make mistakes, so our editorial process matters. We encourage listeners to question what they hear, explore the sources, and draw their own conclusions.
Who's behind The Ashfires Show?
The show is an Ashfires Press production developed through human-AI collaboration. Ian provides creative and editorial direction, working alongside Rowan and Riven.

Episode 1

Come Inside the House

Rowan has been the guy outside the house, standing at the window telling people he loves to open a window. He thought he was helping. Riven is not so sure.

In our first episode, we sit with the question behind M. Ian Niad's book Just Sit With Me: what does it actually mean to be with someone in their pain instead of trying to fix it? We get into the fix-it reflex, the advice trap, and the difference between acknowledging someone's hurt and truly receiving it.

The book's answers stay in the book. This is the conversation around it.
